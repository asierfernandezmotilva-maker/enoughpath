How the calculator works

Each year starts with the withdrawal, and what is left grows at the return after inflation. With a steady return the number of years has a closed formula:

Years = ln(W(1 + r) ÷ (W(1 + r) − P × r)) ÷ ln(1 + r)

P is the portfolio, W the yearly withdrawal and r the real return. When W(1 + r) is no more than P × r, the return pays for the withdrawal and the money lasts indefinitely. With the example values, $40,000 a year from $1,000,000 at 3% lasts 44.1 years.

Why the bad-start test matters

Real returns vary, and losses early in retirement do the most damage, because each withdrawal sells a bigger share of a smaller portfolio. The test assumes the first two years fall 20% and 10% and every later year earns your steady return; with the example values the money lasts 23.9 years instead of 44.1 years. It is a stress test, not a forecast. For two retirees with the same average return in opposite order, see the 4% rule explained.

How long $1,000,000 lasts

The full table by withdrawal from $30,000 to $100,000 and return from 0% to 6% is in how long will $1 million last in retirement. For a retirement of a given length, Morningstar's latest safe starting rates are 3.9% for 30 years and 2.9% for 50 years.

These results come from the numbers you enter and a steady return, not from market history or a forecast. Taxes, fees and changes in spending are not included. See the disclaimer.

Assumptions and limits

  • The withdrawal stays the same in today's dollars every year, taken at the start of the year.
  • The return is the same every year, except in the bad-start test.
  • Taxes and fees are not included; a 1% fee lowers the return by about one point.
  • Social Security, pensions and part-time income are not included. Income that starts later lowers what the portfolio pays from then on.

Questions people ask

How long will $600,000 last in retirement?

It depends on the withdrawal and the return. At a steady 3% after inflation, taking $24,000 a year (4%) lasts 44.1 years and $36,000 (6%) lasts 22.5 years. With two bad years at the start, $24,000 a year lasts 23.9 years.
