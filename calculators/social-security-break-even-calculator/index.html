Social Security break-even calculator

The age at which waiting to claim Social Security pays more in total, with the SSA's reduction and credit rules.

With a $2,000 benefit at 67, claiming at 62 pays $1,400 a month and claiming at 67 pays $2,000. Waiting pays more in total from age 78.7; between 67 and 70 the break-even age is 82.5.

The rules behind it

For anyone born in 1960 or later, full retirement age is 67. Claiming early cuts the benefit by 5/9 of 1% a month for the first 36 months and 5/12 of 1% for each month beyond, so at 62 it is 70% of the full amount. Waiting past 67 adds 2/3 of 1% a month, 8% a year, up to 124% at 70.

The ages do not depend on the size of the benefit, only on the two claiming ages. Cost-of-living adjustments raise both choices alike, so the calculator leaves them out and works in today's dollars. It does not discount future payments or include taxes, spousal benefits or the earnings test.

Questions people ask

What is the Social Security break even point?

The age at which the total received from a later, larger benefit catches up with the total from an earlier, smaller one. Claiming at 67 instead of 62 breaks even at 78.7; live longer than that and waiting paid more.

What is my Social Security break even age?

It depends on the two ages you compare: 78.7 for 62 vs 67, 80.4 for 62 vs 70 and 82.5 for 67 vs 70, under the rules for people born in 1960 or later.
